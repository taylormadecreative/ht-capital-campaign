Why HT? Why now?

A mosaic community of learners, 150 years in the making.

Huston-Tillotson was founded in 1875. We were here before Austin had skyscrapers. Before SXSW. Before the tech companies. Before anyone stood in a two-hour line for barbecue. Before Austin became Austin.

HT is Austin's first institution of higher education and its third oldest business. Over 150 years, one of America's most talked-about cities literally grew up around us, and we are still here: on the same historic hill, looking out over one of the most dynamic economic centers in America.

HT has survived Reconstruction, segregation, world wars, the Great Depression, recessions, social upheaval, technological revolutions, and a global pandemic. Still teaching. Still believing. Still serving. Still standing on this hill.

HT exists to cultivate the forerunners of innovation. Our students come from Texas, California, across America and around the world, and together they form a mosaic community of learners. They become scientists, teachers, entrepreneurs, researchers, business leaders, public servants, and community builders.

But the world doesn't just need smarter people. It needs better people. At HT, education has never been separated from faith, character, and service. We want brilliant scientists who understand ethics, entrepreneurs who understand stewardship, and leaders who know that sometimes the more important question isn't “Can I?” but “Should I?”

Austin isn't simply our address. Austin is our classroom. Students are surrounded by the companies, entrepreneurs, healthcare systems, and industries shaping the future, right outside the front gate.

But rankings aren't the real victory. Students are.

We educate the mind.
We develop the skill.
We nurture the spirit.

And then we send graduates into the world prepared to do something meaningful with all three. When soul, competence, and character walk into the workplace together, the world gets better.

We have something urban universities dream about

Land. On our historic campus, overlooking downtown Austin.

Not somewhere miles outside the city. Not property we hope to buy someday. At a time when urban universities around the country are struggling to find room to expand, HT has the ability to build.

God blessed us with some room on this hill. Now it's our responsibility to be good stewards of it. And maybe, just maybe, HT has been sitting on this land all these years for a moment exactly like this one.

Then came a moment that changed everything

The Moody Foundation made an extraordinary $150 million investment in Huston-Tillotson. The largest single gift ever made to an HBCU.

They looked at our students. Our leadership. Our location. Our plans. Our history. Our possibilities. And they said, “We're in.”

We are profoundly grateful, but our faith teaches us something important about blessings: blessings come with responsibility. What has been entrusted to us isn't simply something to celebrate. It is something to steward. Something to multiply. Something to use in service of others.

Of that gift, $100 million is already set aside to turn the Master Plan into reality.

The Moody investment wasn't the finish line.
It was ignition.

    I.

    Building the Future

    Campus & Master Plan · $300–400 million

    The Master Plan was completed in 2025 and fully funded by the United Methodist Church's Black College Fund. HT controls developable land on its historic campus in downtown Austin, and in one of America's most dynamic urban markets, land is usually the obstacle to transformational campus development.

    • Student Housing + Retail Village, approx. 800 beds $100–140M
    • The HT Arena, 5,500 seats $100–150M
    • Academic Building + Student Union $75–100M
    • Preserve Bluebonnet Hill $25–40M
    • Center for Philanthropy & Innovation $35–50M
    II.

    Finishing What We Start

    Student Success & Scholarships · $175–225 million

    Buildings change campuses. Scholarships change families. No academically successful HT student should reach the final two years and have money be the reason they cannot finish.

    • Meet Me Halfway, expanding Ross Moody Finishing the Last Mile $125–175M
    • Ram Training Camp, the six-week summer bridge $30–40M
    • Student Scholarship, Persistence & Opportunity Fund $15–25M
    “Work hard. Stay focused. Complete your sophomore year, and the generosity of the friends of HT will cover the tuition for your junior and senior year.”
    III.

    Creating What Comes Next

    Academic Innovation & Entrepreneurship · $75–100 million

    An Academic Innovation Endowment for new programs, faculty development, laboratories, technology, certifications, and emerging workforce initiatives, so a new academic idea doesn't have to compete for already limited operating dollars.

    HT founded and hosts the HBCU AI Conference & Training Summit. The inaugural gathering drew representatives from 20 HBCUs and more than 400 attendees; the 2026 summit exceeded 600. Among surveyed HBCU students, 98% already use AI and 60% want formal AI coursework.

    HT shouldn't simply talk about the future. HT should have the resources to teach it.

    IV.

    Forever HT

    Endowment & Institutional Strength · $100–150 million

    The most transformative campaigns do two things at once: they change what you can see, and strengthen what you cannot. A substantial unrestricted endowment gives future HT leaders the resources to respond to opportunities no one today can predict.

    HT should never again have to choose between having a great idea and having the resources to pursue it.

    Buildings are visible. An endowment is endurance.
